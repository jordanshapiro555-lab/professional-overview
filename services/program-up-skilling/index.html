Training focus areas

Every module uses your real funnels, backlog, and data. No generic case studies or theoretical exercises—your team practices on the problems they will actually solve.

💡

Hypothesis quality and evidence standards

Move from "let's test a new button color" to evidence-backed hypotheses grounded in behavioral data, analytics insights, and UX principles. Learn to write hypotheses that are specific, measurable, and tied to a behavioral mechanism.

🔬

Test design, QA, and statistical validity

Learn to design experiments that actually answer the question: proper sample sizing, runtime calculations, metric hierarchy, variant isolation, and pre-launch QA workflows that catch errors before they burn traffic.

📊

Analysis, interpretation, and decision confidence

Go beyond "is it significant?" to understanding practical significance, segment-level effects, novelty decay, and when to ship, iterate, or kill an experiment. Build decision frameworks your team trusts.

👥

Program rituals and cross-functional alignment

Establish intake processes, sprint cadences, readout formats, and stakeholder communication rhythms. Build the operational muscle that turns a collection of ad-hoc tests into a compounding program.

📑

Templates, playbooks, and operating documentation

Walk away with reusable hypothesis templates, QA checklists, analysis frameworks, experiment readout decks, and governance playbooks—all customized to your tools and workflows.

Signs your team is ready for up-skilling

You do not need to be starting from zero. These are the patterns that signal an up-skilling engagement will have the highest impact.

⚠️

You rely on an external agency to run experiments — your team wants to build in-house capability but does not have the frameworks or confidence yet.

⚠️

Test quality is inconsistent — some team members write strong hypotheses while others launch "gut feel" tests with vague success criteria.

⚠️

Analysis stops at "winner or loser" — your team can read a p-value but struggles with segment analysis, practical significance, or connecting results to business decisions.

⚠️

You are building a new CRO function or pod — leadership has funded the team but nobody has established the operating playbook or quality standards.

⚠️

Experimentation knowledge lives in one person's head — if that person leaves, the institutional knowledge and operational rigor goes with them.

⚠️

Stakeholders don't trust experiment results — leadership questions methodology, cherry-picked metrics, or "inconclusive" outcomes and pushes back on the program.

How up-skilling works

A structured, four-phase model designed to build real capability—not just deliver content. Your team practices on their own data, with coaching until the skills stick.

1

Assess

Baseline team maturity across hypothesis writing, test design, analytics proficiency, and program operations. Identify the specific capability gaps that will have the biggest impact on experimentation quality and velocity.

2

Train

Deliver live, hands-on workshops using your real funnels, backlog, and analytics data. Each session builds a specific skill with guided exercises, live feedback, and practical takeaways your team can apply the same week.

3

Coach

Embed real-time feedback loops during active experiments. Review hypotheses, analysis plans, and readouts with the team as they work—correcting in context rather than in theory.

4

Sustain

Deliver operating playbooks, templates, and documented standards. Run a final "graduation" sprint where the team executes a full experiment cycle independently with advisory-only support.

Frequently asked questions

How many training sessions are included?

A typical engagement includes 4–6 live workshop sessions delivered over 4–8 weeks, plus ongoing coaching check-ins during active experiments. Scope is adjusted based on team size, starting maturity, and priority skill gaps.

Is training delivered virtually or in person?

Both options are available. Most engagements are delivered virtually via live workshops with screen sharing, collaborative exercises, and real-time feedback. In-person sessions can be arranged for teams that prefer hands-on, co-located training.

What if our team has mixed experience levels?

The curriculum adapts to your team's composition. I assess individual skill gaps during the baseline phase so workshop content challenges experienced practitioners while remaining accessible and valuable for newer team members.

Will we get templates and playbooks we can keep?

Yes. Every engagement includes reusable hypothesis documentation templates, QA checklists, analysis frameworks, experiment readout formats, and governance playbooks—all customized to your tools, workflows, and organizational context.

How do you measure whether the training worked?

We set measurable skill benchmarks at the start (e.g., hypothesis quality rubric scores, QA error rates, analysis depth). Post-engagement, you can track these same metrics to see improvement. I also run a capstone sprint where the team executes independently.
