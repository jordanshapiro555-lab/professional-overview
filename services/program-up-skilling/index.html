What your team practices

Training is built around the work your team already has to do, not abstract examples that disappear after the workshop.

1

Hypothesis and evidence standards

How to use analytics, user behavior, page review, and business context to write hypotheses that are specific and testable.

2

Experiment design fundamentals

How to define the primary metric, supporting metrics, sample needs, runtime, targeting, and QA risks before launch.

3

Analysis and readout judgment

How to interpret outcomes, explain confidence, handle mixed signals, and recommend a practical next action.

4

Templates and team rituals

Reusable formats for briefs, QA, readouts, prioritization, and retros so better habits survive after the coaching ends.

Training model

The coaching model mirrors the three skill gaps above.

The engagement turns your real CRO work into practice. Each training lane maps to one reason teams invest: stronger hypotheses, cleaner experiment design, and readouts that create decision confidence.

Hypothesis lab

Teams practice turning evidence into a clear behavior-change mechanism, expected outcome, and learning question.

  • Evidence synthesis
  • Mechanism clarity
  • Testable prediction

Experiment design clinic

Teams pressure-test the brief before launch so the metric, guardrails, runtime, targeting, and QA plan support a real decision.

  • Metric hierarchy
  • Variant isolation
  • QA risk review

Readout coaching

Teams practice explaining what happened, what the result does and does not prove, and what action should come next.

  • Result interpretation
  • Decision confidence
  • Next-test logic

Signs this is the right starting point

Up-skilling works best when the team is close enough to execute but needs stronger judgment, standards, and confidence.

H

Hypotheses are inconsistent. Some ideas are evidence-backed, while others are vague changes without a clear behavior mechanism.

Q

QA issues create doubt. The team worries about targeting, tracking, or variant quality after tests are already live.

A

Readouts stop too soon. Results say winner or loser, but they do not explain what the team should do next.

O

Ownership is concentrated in one person. The program depends on one expert instead of shared team standards.

Up-skilling questions, answered

What does CRO up-skilling include?

It includes hands-on training, coaching, templates, and feedback on real experiments so the team improves hypothesis quality, test design, analysis, and decision-making.

Is the training generic or customized?

It is customized around your funnels, backlog, tools, data, and team maturity. The goal is practical skill transfer, not a generic CRO lecture.

Who should join the sessions?

Growth, marketing, product, UX, analytics and experimentation partners can all participate. The best mix depends on who owns hypotheses, launches, readouts and decisions.

Will the team get templates to keep?

Yes. The engagement can include hypothesis templates, QA checklists, analysis readout formats, prioritization rubrics and coaching notes adapted to your workflow.

How do we know the training worked?

We define practical markers up front, such as stronger hypothesis quality, fewer QA gaps, clearer readouts, and more confident experiment decisions from the internal team.
