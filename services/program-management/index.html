What gets managed

Signs this is the right starting point

Program management is useful when your team has momentum, but the operating layer is slowing everything down.

Q

Experiments stall in queues. Good ideas wait too long for design, development, QA, analytics, or stakeholder approval.

G

Governance is unclear. Teams compete for test slots, and priority is decided by urgency instead of evidence.

R

Readouts do not change decisions. Results are shared, but the roadmap does not clearly move from what was learned.

L

Learning disappears. Past tests are hard to find, hard to trust, or disconnected from the next wave of ideas.

Program management questions, answered

What does CRO program management include?

It includes experiment intake, prioritization, sprint planning, QA standards, launch monitoring, reporting, and learning documentation so the testing program runs with less friction.

Who is this service for?

It is for teams that already have testing ideas, tools, and stakeholders but need a stronger operating system to keep experiments moving and decisions clear.

Do you replace our product, design, or engineering teams?

No. I coordinate the experimentation workflow across those teams so each partner has clearer briefs, cleaner handoffs, and fewer avoidable blockers.

Can this work with our current tools?

Yes. I adapt the operating cadence to your existing stack, whether the team uses Jira, Asana, Notion, spreadsheets, an A/B testing platform, or analytics dashboards.

What happens after the engagement?

You keep the intake model, QA checklists, readout format, reporting cadence, and governance habits so the program can keep running internally.
