What gets managed

Every operational lever that determines whether your experimentation program ships fast, analyzes cleanly, and proves value to leadership.

📋

Experiment intake and prioritization governance

A structured intake process that filters ideas by evidence quality, expected impact, and strategic alignment. No more "HiPPO-driven" backlogs or pet projects jumping the queue.

📅

Sprint planning, sequencing, and dependency management

Bi-weekly sprint planning that balances capacity, traffic allocation, and cross-team dependencies. Every sprint has clear goals, owners, and deadlines.

✅

QA standards and launch readiness checks

Pre-launch QA checklists covering targeting, analytics tagging, variant rendering, and goal configuration. Catch errors before they burn traffic and invalidate results.

👥

Cross-functional workflow orchestration

Coordinate across product, design, engineering, and analytics so handoffs are clean and no experiment stalls waiting for a review, build, or data pull.

📈

Weekly leadership reporting tied to KPIs

Transparent readouts with win/loss/learning summaries, pipeline velocity, and program ROI metrics. Leadership sees the business impact—not just a list of experiments.

📚

Knowledge management and learning repository

Structured documentation of every experiment: hypothesis, results, decision, and downstream implications. Institutional learning compounds instead of disappearing.

Signs your program needs operational leadership

You have the tools and the talent. What's missing is the system that ties them together.

⚠️

Tests are stuck in a queue for weeks — experiments wait for design, dev, or QA sign-off and cycle times are unpredictable.

⚠️

Stakeholders compete for testing slots — without governance, the loudest team gets priority regardless of expected impact.

⚠️

Analysis is inconsistent or delayed — tests run to completion but results take weeks to analyze and decisions stall.

⚠️

Leadership questions the program's ROI — you know the program has value, but you can't report it in terms the C-suite understands.

⚠️

QA errors waste traffic and credibility — experiments launch with targeting bugs, broken analytics, or rendering issues that only surface mid-test.

⚠️

Learnings evaporate between tests — nobody documents what was tested, what was learned, or why a decision was made six months ago.

Management cadence

A repeatable operating rhythm that creates accountability, removes blockers, and keeps the program visible to leadership.

1

Plan

Align sprint goals with business KPIs and available capacity. Prioritize the backlog using an evidence-based framework so the highest-impact experiments ship first. Define success criteria before any experiment starts.

2

Execute

Coordinate build, QA, and deployment across product, design, engineering, and analytics. Run pre-launch readiness checks, monitor live experiments for data quality issues, and escalate blockers before they stall the sprint.

3

Report

Publish weekly readouts with clear win/loss decisions, statistical context, and next actions. Monthly summaries tie program velocity and cumulative impact to the revenue and conversion KPIs leadership cares about.

Frequently asked questions

How many tests can you manage per month?

It depends on team size, traffic volume, and technical capacity—but most programs I manage run 4–8 concurrent experiments once the operating cadence is established. The goal is sustainable velocity, not test count inflation.

What tools do you use for program management?

I adapt to your existing stack. Common setups include Jira, Asana, or Notion for experiment tracking alongside your A/B testing platform (Optimizely, VWO, AB Tasty, etc.) and analytics suite. I do not force a tool change.

Do you manage the developers and designers too?

I coordinate the workflow across product, design, engineering, and analytics—but I do not replace those functions. I run the operating process so your specialists can focus on building and analyzing, not chasing status updates.

How does the handoff work at the end of an engagement?

You receive documented SOPs, templates, reporting frameworks, and governance playbooks. I also offer structured handoff sessions with your internal team to ensure continuity. The goal is a self-sustaining program, not a dependency.

What's the typical engagement length?

Most program management engagements run 3–6 months. That is enough time to establish the cadence, prove ROI, and build internal muscle. Some teams extend to 12 months for continuous operation and optimization.
