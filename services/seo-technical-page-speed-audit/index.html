Signs this is the right starting point

A page speed audit is most useful when performance problems are visible, but the team needs clarity on which issues are worth fixing first.

C

Core Web Vitals are unstable. Your scores vary by template, device, or traffic source and the cause is not obvious.

S

SEO pages are underperforming. Important pages have rankings or traffic potential but load slowly or feel heavy.

E

Engineering needs sharper direction. The backlog has performance ideas, but not enough impact context to sequence them.

U

Users lose momentum. Landing pages, product pages, or forms feel sluggish at the exact moment visitors need confidence.

Page speed audit questions, answered

What does a page speed audit include?

The audit includes Core Web Vitals review, page template diagnosis, technical bottleneck identification, UX and conversion implications, and a prioritized implementation roadmap.

Who is this audit for?

It is for ecommerce, SaaS, lead generation, and content teams that rely on organic traffic or paid landing pages and need faster experiences that support ranking, usability, and conversion.

What inputs are useful?

Useful inputs include priority URLs, page templates, Search Console data, GA4 data, PageSpeed Insights or Lighthouse reports, known engineering constraints, and recent site changes.

Does this include implementation?

The audit is built to make implementation easier, but engineering execution can be scoped separately after the roadmap is reviewed.

How is this different from a generic Lighthouse report?

The audit translates speed data into business-facing priorities, separating quick wins from larger technical fixes and explaining how each issue affects SEO, UX, and conversion.
