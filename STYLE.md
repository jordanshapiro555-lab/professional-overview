# Jordan's CRO Studio — Writing Style Guide

The Writer agent MUST follow every rule below. The QA agent MUST verify them.

## Voice
- Direct, practitioner-first. Write like you're explaining a test result to a smart client over coffee.
- First-person plural ("we tested...") when describing methodology. Second-person ("you") when giving advice.
- No hedging filler: cut "I think," "perhaps," "it could be argued."

## Sentence rules
- Average sentence length: 14–20 words.
- Hard cap: no sentence over 30 words.
- At least 1 sentence under 8 words per section (creates rhythm).

## Structure rules
- Every H2 section MUST contain at least 1 cited data point (study, benchmark, or test result with a number and a source).
- Every section ends with a concrete takeaway, not a summary.
- Use bulleted lists only when sequence doesn't matter. Use numbered lists for steps.

## Banned jargon (rewrite or cut)
- "leverage" → use "use"
- "synergy," "synergistic" → cut
- "best-in-class" → cut
- "robust solution" → cut
- "game-changer" → cut
- "delve," "delving," "delves" → cut
- "in today's fast-paced world" → cut entire phrase
- "unlock," "unleash" (as marketing verbs) → cut
- "seamless," "seamlessly" → cut unless describing literal UX
- "moving forward" → use "next"
- "at the end of the day" → cut
- "circle back" → cut
- "low-hanging fruit" → use the actual specific opportunity

## Required elements per post
- 1 contrarian or non-obvious take in the intro (something most CRO content gets wrong).
- 1 specific test example with: hypothesis, variant, metric, lift %, sample size.
- 1 quote or stat from Baymard, NNG, CXL, GoodUI, or peer-reviewed source.
- 1 internal link to another /blog/ post.
- 1 closing CTA that invites a specific next action (audit, conversation, subscribe).

## Banned patterns
- No "In this post, we'll explore..." intros. Start with the insight.
- No "In conclusion" or "To summarize" closers.
- No em-dash overuse — use them sparingly (max 2 per post).
- No rhetorical questions in headings ("Why does this matter?"). Make a claim instead.
