How I drove a 11.9% Home CTR lift via social proof redesign

A slimmer, more aesthetically pleasing social proof section more effectively earned user trust

Polishing the social proof section's design — without changing the underlying content — lifted homepage CTR by 11.9%.

Return to winners Contact me

The Challenge

Dated aesthetics in the social proof section were undermining brand credibility

UHOne's homepage social proof block had accumulated design debt over time — oversized typography, inconsistent spacing, and a visual weight that clashed with the rest of the page. In health insurance, trust signals are only persuasive when the presentation itself communicates quality. A social proof section that looks like an afterthought sends the wrong signal at a critical moment in the user's decision process. The hypothesis was that a cleaner, more intentional redesign would convert the same underlying proof points more effectively.

Hypothesis

If we redesign the social proof section with a slimmer, more aesthetically polished layout, then homepage CTR will improve because users interpret design quality as a proxy for brand credibility — a well-crafted trust signal is more persuasive than the same content presented poorly.

Key Insight

💡

Design aesthetics act as trust signals independently of the content they frame. Users interpret a well-crafted social proof block as evidence that the brand itself is well-run, making them more willing to engage. The 11.9% lift — nearly double what the product grid redesign achieved — confirms that trust optimisation has outsized top-of-funnel impact in categories with high purchase anxiety like health insurance.

This insight informed the Forbes Award social proof test, which layered a third-party credential on top of the redesigned section and delivered an additional 3.4% desktop Quote Start Rate lift.
