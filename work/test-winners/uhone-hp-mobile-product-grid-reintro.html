How I drove a 4.5% Quote Start Rate lift via mobile product grid re-introduction

Reducing the hero size and re-introducing a clickable product grid on the UHOne mobile homepage drove a 4.5% lift in Quote Start Rate and a 16% lift in Cart Additions per visit

Re-introducing the product grid mobile users had lost in a prior redesign restored a critical browsing pathway — driving a 4.5% QSR lift and 16% more cart additions.

Return to winners Contact me

The Challenge

A previous mobile redesign removed the product grid — and with it, the ability to browse

At some point in UHOne's site evolution, the mobile homepage was redesigned to feature a full-screen hero that consumed the entire viewport. The product grid — which had allowed users to browse available plans directly — was removed to create a cleaner aesthetic. While visually minimalist, this change forced all mobile users into a single funnel path: interact with the hero CTA. Users who had arrived with a specific product in mind, or who preferred to browse before committing to a form, had no alternative. Mobile bounce rates and low product engagement suggested a meaningful proportion of users were being turned away by the absence of a browsable product layer.

Hypothesis

If we reduce the mobile hero size and re-introduce a clickable product grid below it, then mobile Quote Start Rate and Cart Additions will improve because users who prefer to browse products before starting a Census form will have an alternative entry path that better matches their intent and browsing behaviour.

Key Insight

💡

The 16% cart additions lift — much larger than the QSR lift — confirms that a meaningful segment of mobile users were ready to add products directly without going through the full Census form. The product grid re-introduction didn't just create a new path: it restored a shortcut that had been removed. This is a cautionary reminder that removing features for aesthetic reasons, without sufficient behavioural data to support the trade-off, can quietly erode conversion performance over time.

This finding reinforced the value of the product grid that had also been validated on desktop in the Product Grid Redesign test, confirming the pattern holds across device types.
