How I drove an 11% cart additions lift via additional product intent capture

Adding a new product intent capture feature globally enabled user pathing choice throughout the experience, leading to easier funnel progression and more appropriate product displays down-funnel

A global sticky nav that captures product intent gave ready-to-convert users a faster path — and fed downstream personalisation — driving an 11% lift in cart additions site-wide.

Return to winners Contact me

The Challenge

Users who knew what they wanted had no global mechanism to act on that intent

UHOne's funnel was designed around a sequential flow: homepage → Census form → product list → cart. Users who already knew which product they wanted — returning visitors, users who'd done outside research — were forced into the same linear path as first-time explorers. This created friction and unnecessary funnel steps for a segment of high-intent users who had already made their decision. Additionally, without any way to capture product preference early, the site had no basis for personalising the downstream experience. The opportunity was to create a global intent-capture mechanism that would both shortcut ready-to-convert users and feed personalisation signals to the rest of the funnel.

Hypothesis

If we add a product intent capture feature to a global sticky navigation bar, then cart additions will increase because users who already know which product they want can express that intent immediately from any page, bypassing unnecessary funnel steps, and the captured intent signal will improve product display relevance downstream.

Key Insight

💡

Adding a product intent signal globally served two purposes: it gave ready-to-convert users a faster path to cart, and it fed the funnel with better-qualified intent data that improved product display relevance downstream. The 11% cart addition lift reflects both mechanisms working together. This test validated a core principle: not all users need or want a guided funnel — some just need the door to their destination. Giving them that door is one of the highest-leverage changes available in complex insurance funnels.

The success of this test immediately prompted a fast-follow iteration to test whether simplifying the feature's CTA structure could compound the gains — which it did, adding another 2.1% CTR lift.
