How I drove a 2.1% Quote Start Rate lift via American idealism hero imagery

Swapping the UHOne homepage hero to imagery aligned with American values drove a 2.1% lift in Quote Start Rate and a statistically significant 5.3% lift in Quote Submit Rate

Replacing generic stock imagery with photography that reflects American values drove a 2.1% QSR lift — and a larger 5.3% lift in Quote Submit Rate downstream.

Return to winners Contact me

The Challenge

Generic stock photography wasn't creating an emotional connection with UHOne's core audience

UHOne's health insurance audience skews toward Americans who hold patriotic values, family-centric worldviews, and a sense of personal responsibility — particularly middle America demographics. The existing homepage hero relied on generic stock photography that failed to speak to this identity. Images of generic people in generic settings create no emotional resonance; they're visually neutral at best, and can subtly communicate that the brand doesn't understand its audience. In the health insurance category, where trust and shared values drive purchase decisions, imagery that reflects the customer's identity is a meaningful differentiator.

Hypothesis

If we replace the generic homepage hero with photography evoking American idealism — family, community, the American dream, then Quote Start Rate will improve because users who see themselves and their values reflected in the brand's visual identity are more emotionally engaged and more likely to take the first step toward purchasing.

Key Insight

💡

The larger lift downstream — Quote Submit at +5.3% versus Quote Start at +2.1% — suggests the imagery didn't just drive more funnel entries, it attracted a more motivated audience. Users who saw themselves in the imagery carried that emotional alignment deeper into the funnel, converting at a higher rate at every subsequent step. This is the compound power of identity-based marketing: it's both more persuasive at the top of funnel and more selective about who enters it.
