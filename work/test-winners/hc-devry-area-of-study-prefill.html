How I drove a 17.11% lead form completion lift via area of study pre-fill

Pre-filling the Area of Study field based on the program page a user came from removed the single biggest point of hesitation in DeVry's lead capture funnel.

Contextual pre-fill turned a decision point into a confirmation — and drove a 17.11% lift in lead form completions across program-specific landing pages.

Return to winners Contact me

The Challenge

Users arriving from program pages still had to manually select their area of study — at the moment they were closest to converting

DeVry University's lead generation funnel directed users from program-specific landing pages — Accounting & Finance, Technology, Healthcare — straight into a multi-step inquiry form. Despite the fact that users had self-selected their interest by navigating to a program page, the lead form started cold: the Area of Study dropdown was blank, requiring users to re-select what they had effectively already communicated. For prospective students already dealing with a high-consideration decision, this redundant task introduced friction at exactly the wrong moment. Drop-off analysis showed the Area of Study field was the leading cause of form abandonment on step one, and that abandonment was disproportionately high among mobile users navigating a dropdown on a small screen.

Hypothesis

If we pre-fill the Area of Study field in the lead form based on the program landing page the user arrived from, then lead form completion rate will improve because eliminating the need to re-select an interest the user already demonstrated reduces cognitive load and removes the primary friction point at form step one.

Test Design

PagesAll DeVry program-specific landing pages with embedded lead form
DevicesDesktop & Mobile
AudienceAll landing page visitors who reached the lead form
Traffic Split50/50 control (blank field) / variant (pre-filled field)
Duration~3 months

Key Insight

💡

Pre-fill isn't just a convenience feature — it's a form of recognition. When a user arrives on an Accounting & Finance program page and sees "Accounting & Finance" already selected in the form, the page communicates: "we know why you're here." That acknowledgment reduces hesitation and signals that the process will be straightforward. The 17.11% lift reflected users who were qualified and motivated but were being lost to a redundant interaction — re-selecting an interest they had already expressed by navigating to a program page. Removing that friction was enough to move the needle meaningfully at scale.

Pre-fill simply captured intent that was already there. The lift came not from changing user motivation, but from removing a small unnecessary barrier at the most critical moment in the funnel.
