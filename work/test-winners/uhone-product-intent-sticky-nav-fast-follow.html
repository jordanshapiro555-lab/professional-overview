How I drove a 2.1% CTR lift via simplified CTA layout

Reducing choice with a single CTA improved CTR by 2.1% globally

Iterating on the winning product intent nav to replace multiple CTAs with a single focused action removed a micro-friction that had been suppressing click-through.

Return to winners Contact me

The Challenge

The original product intent feature presented multiple CTAs — and users were hesitating between them

The first iteration of the product intent sticky nav (see: Product Intent Capture via Sticky Nav) was a clear winner at +11% cart additions. But post-test interaction analysis revealed a subtle friction point: users were engaging with the feature — hovering, pausing — but not clicking at the rate the feature's position and prominence would suggest. The pattern pointed to a micro-decision fatigue problem. The multi-CTA layout was presenting users who had already decided to act with a secondary choice they weren't expecting. The fast-follow question was simple: does a single focused CTA remove this last hesitation and improve throughput?

Hypothesis

If we simplify the product intent sticky nav to a single primary CTA instead of multiple competing options, then global CTR on the product intent feature will improve because removing the forced secondary choice from an already-committed user eliminates the last micro-friction before action.

Key Insight

💡

This is a textbook example of iterative CRO: the initial winner (the product intent sticky nav) had a secondary friction point that was masked by the primary lift. The fast-follow identified and eliminated it. The relatively modest 2.1% lift reflects the fact that this was optimising an already-winning experience — a much harder task than the original improvement. But compounded with the original +11% cart additions test, the net impact of the two tests together is meaningfully larger than either alone. Choice architecture matters even within a feature: two CTAs of equal visual weight cause hesitation; one focused CTA produces decisive action.
