How I drove a 6.6% cart additions lift via dynamic pop-up

Because not all health insurance products are sold in all states, users would often get to plan lists and be unable to find the product they were initially shopping for, resulting in confusion and high drop-off. I remedied this with a dynamic pop-up that both set more appropriate expectations and confirmed for users that UHOne was in fact using Census data, which also improved form fill.

A proactive product availability notification at the Census form step reduced confusion-driven drop-off and lifted cart additions by 6.6%.

Return to winners Contact me

The Challenge

Users were discovering product unavailability deep in the funnel — after investing significant effort

UHOne's health insurance products vary by state — not all plans are available in every region. Users who had entered their zip code into the Census form and navigated through to the plan listing step would sometimes discover that the specific product they'd come to purchase wasn't available in their state. With no prior warning, this felt like a broken experience: they'd done the work, and the site appeared to have nothing for them. The result was high drop-off at the plan listing stage and a perception of product scarcity that damaged trust. The opportunity was to surface this information earlier — before users invested more effort — and frame it as a helpful feature rather than a failure.

Hypothesis

If we add a dynamic pop-up at the Census form that proactively communicates state-based product availability and confirms that UHOne uses verified Census data, then cart additions and form fill rate will improve because users who understand the system are more willing to trust and proceed, while users in non-qualifying regions self-select out earlier rather than dropping off at a more costly funnel stage.

Key Insight

💡

Expectation management before a potential disappointment is more effective than damage control after it. By surfacing the availability check earlier and framing it as a data-accuracy feature (Census data confirmation), users gained confidence rather than confusion. The dual lift in both cart additions and form completions confirms the pop-up worked on two audiences simultaneously: qualifying users who proceeded with greater confidence, and non-qualifying users who opted out earlier rather than abandoning mid-funnel.

This test paired well with the Census new-tab test, which addressed a different form friction: the cognitive conflict between researching products and filling out the form at the same time.
