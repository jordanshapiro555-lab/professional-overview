How I lifted product views with sticky sort and filter controls

Mirroring a familiar app pattern made sort and filter controls easier to access on mobile PLPs, improving product exploration and creating strong engagement lifts on refinement behaviors.

The recommended app-mimicking variation lifted product view rate by 0.23%, increased ATC from PDP by 0.64%, and produced a 55.21% lift in sort feature engagement.

Return to winners Contact me

The Challenge

Mobile shoppers needed easier access to browsing tools on PLPs

Aeropostale shoppers browsing mobile product listing pages had access to sort and filter tools, but the web interaction pattern did not make those controls as persistent or as app-like as users expected. The opportunity was to make refinement feel more immediate, familiar, and available during product discovery.

Hypothesis

If we mirror the high-performing app sticky sort and filter experience on mobile PLPs, then product view rate and add-to-cart behavior will improve because users can refine the product grid more easily and reach relevant products faster.

Key Insight

Separating sort and filter clarified two different browsing jobs: ordering products and narrowing the set. The app-mimicking variant did not create a dramatic purchase lift on its own, but it produced the cleanest positive movement across product views, ATC from PDP, product clicks, checkout, and refinement engagement. That made it the right winner to carry into the next sort and filter iteration.
