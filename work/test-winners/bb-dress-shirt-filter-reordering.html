How I improved PLP engagement by reordering dress shirt filters

Brooks Brothers dress shirt shoppers were showing clear search demand for fabric and collection language, so the PLP filter order needed to match how users actually looked for shirts.

Surfacing important filters earlier encouraged feature engagement by raising Fabric and Collections while deprioritizing Size below Pattern, where it created more upper-funnel confusion.

Return to winnersContact me

The Challenge

The default filter order did not reflect how dress shirt shoppers searched

Search behavior showed strong demand for terms tied to fabric, patterns, occasions, and collections. Linen appeared in more than 29,000 searches over the previous 12 months, and Oxford was the eighth most common term with more than 18,000 searches.

Hypothesis

If we move high-demand dress shirt filters earlier in the PLP refinement experience, then CTR and filter engagement will improve because users can immediately interact with attributes that match their shopping language and search intent.
