How I drove a 24.2% Hero CTA engagement lift via a simplified layout

Simplifying the form by removing the toggle feature and creating single-click pathing improved engagement by 24%

Removing a pre-click toggle and replacing it with a single direct CTA drove a 24.2% lift in hero engagement — the largest in this portfolio.

Return to winners Contact me

The Challenge

A pre-click toggle was creating friction at the moment of highest intent

The HealthMarkets homepage hero CTA asked users to choose between "Individual" and "Family" plans via a toggle before they could proceed to get a quote. This upfront decision, while intended to improve downstream routing, introduced hesitation at the most critical moment of the user journey — the very first interaction. Users who were ready to engage were being slowed down by a forced categorisation they hadn't come prepared to make. The opportunity was to test whether removing this friction entirely would improve top-of-funnel entry, allowing the system to route users downstream rather than at the door.

Hypothesis

If we replace the hero toggle with a single direct CTA requiring no pre-selection, then hero CTA engagement rate will improve because eliminating the mandatory upfront choice reduces cognitive load and capitalises on user momentum at the moment of peak intent.

Key Insight

💡

The toggle was optimising for routing accuracy at the direct cost of conversion rate — a classic tension in funnel design. In most cases, getting users into the funnel and letting downstream logic handle routing produces better overall results than asking users to self-select before they're ready. The 24.2% lift confirms that first-click simplicity beats first-click precision.

This principle of single-path CTAs was later applied to the UHOne product intent sticky nav fast-follow test, which similarly found that reducing choice at a conversion action improved clickthrough.
