Connecting The UHOne Shopping Journey To Unlock $16.77M In Projected Annualized Value

UHOne was attracting strong traffic but losing buyers at the post-Census product display. By capturing product intent at the top of the page and carrying it downstream, the program shipped 3 winners and $16.77M in projected annualized incremental value.

Executive Summary

UHOne was attracting strong traffic to Home and Product pages, but Census form completion and post-Census product fit were leaving material revenue on the table. A structured CRO program focused on capturing product intent early and carrying it downstream produced 3 shipped winners and $16.77M in projected annualized value.

  • $16.77M: projected annualized value shipped
  • +11.4%: Cart Additions per Visitor lift on the top winner (alpha .04)
  • $5.95M: value from #1 winner — Global Product Intent Capture via Sticky Nav
  • $2.63M: value from #2 winner — Home Hero CTA + Product Grid

Three principles shaped the program: intent capture worked (lifted Quote Start and Cart Additions without hurting downstream funnel health), enhanced not retired (the top winner was iterated into a simpler variant that preserved the lift), and upper funnel still matters (trust modules on Product pages drove return visits and compounded conversion over time).

Client Context

UHOne was winning at traffic — and losing at fit.

  • Traffic was landing on Home and Product pages. Visitors were funneled into an ecom flow gated by a Census form, which ensures users only see plans available in their state/ZIP.
  • Census was doing its job, but not its best job. Users frequently arrived, completed Census, then dropped off at the product display step because the assortment didn't match what they came looking for.
  • The journey was disconnected. Nothing about a visitor's stated interest carried from the top of the page into the funnel, so the post-Census experience felt generic instead of personal.

The goal: improve three KPIs in order — Quote Start Rate (visitors entering the Census funnel), Quote Submit Rate (users completing the Census form), and Cart Additions / Purchase (users adding plans and buying).

The Opportunity

We didn't start with a guess — we started with converging evidence. Three independent signals all pointed at the same opportunity:

  1. Site analytics — the biggest drop-off: The step right after Census completion (when users were first shown products) was the single highest drop-off point in the UHOne purchase journey.
  2. VoC research — customers couldn't find fit: Voice-of-Customer data surfaced a consistent complaint: shoppers couldn't see or find the product they came to UHOne to shop for.
  3. Historic testing — intent equals lift: Prior-year tests that captured product intent and used it downstream showed lifts of 7% to 35% — a wide range, but the direction was unambiguous.

The thesis: capture product intent early, and the entire funnel downstream works harder.

Our Approach

A disciplined, evidence-led testing engine — four steps, run as a repeatable cadence rather than a one-time burst:

  1. Strategic audit: Synthesized historic UHOne testing, site analytics, VoC, industry trends and direct-competitor experiences to build a point of view on where to focus.
  2. Pipeline generation: Translated the audit into a structured idea pipeline scoped by page, funnel stage, and user type — Home, Product, Census, and post-Census.
  3. ICE prioritization: Every idea scored on Impact, Confidence and Ease. Highest ICE-score tests shipped first to compound learnings quickly.
  4. Ship & productionize: Tests that demonstrated statistically significant improvement were productionized — code migrated from the testing platform to AEM so wins persisted.

The result: a repeatable cadence that turns research into shipped code.

Strategy

Audit insights translated into two complementary strategic bets, run in parallel:

  1. Create a connected shopping experience. Capture product intent at the top of the page and use it to personalize content and product displays downstream.
    • Add a sticky-nav module that captures product type + ZIP before Census
    • Surface ZIP availability early so users know whether their product of interest is available before they complete Census
    • Pass intent into the post-Census product display to match assortment to stated need
    • Iterate the module shape and copy to reduce friction while preserving lift
  2. Match content to user intent by page. Address high-intent returners on Home and new visitors landing on Product pages with strategically upper- and top-of-funnel content.
    • Home Hero CTA + Product Grid for returning high-intent users
    • "Why United?" trust module on Product pages for new visitors who need a reason to come back
    • Dermatology section to expand MHCP product relevance
    • Census-page intent capture variants to de-risk the #1 bet across placements

Business Impact

$16.77M in projected annualized incremental value across the program — ranked by total projected value:

  • #1 Pre-shop Global Sticky Nav Product Intent Capture: $5.95M
  • #1b Simplified Sticky Nav (enhanced variant): $2.79M
  • #2 Home Hero CTA + Product Grid: $2.63M
  • Census — Desktop Male Hero Image: $3.58M
  • Product Pages New module: $686K
  • Product Pages Exit Intent: $722K
  • Census Product Hero Image: $248K
  • Bundles More Popular Telehealth Pairs Section: $140K
  • MHCP Added Dermatology Section: $14K

Winning Tests

Winner #1 — Sticky Nav Product Intent + ZIP Capture

Hypothesis. A persistent, low-friction sticky nav that asks "what are you shopping for?" plus ZIP would set correct expectations and route users to relevant product displays — increasing Quote Starts and Cart Additions without hurting Census completion.

Result. +11.4% Cart Additions per Visitor lift (1.05% control vs 1.17% test, alpha .04). Shipped to 100% of traffic and enhanced into a simplified variant.

Winner #1 — Results Detail

Variant B (Intent + ZIP) wins on Quote Start Rate (11.45% vs 10.57%) and Cart Additions (1.17% vs 1.05%). Variant C (Intent only, no ZIP) also lifts Cart Additions — showing product intent alone is valuable, but ZIP adds real lift on Quote Starts. Bounce Rate held or improved across both variants — zero guardrail damage.

Why it matters: 4.4% drop in broad Census visits on Variant B (56.97% → 54.46%, statistically significant). Users aren't disappearing — they're being routed into the right Census path (dental, vision, short-term, etc.) the first time. That's the whole thesis in one chart.

Winner #1 Enhanced — Simplified Sticky Nav

We iterated the winner — kept the lift, cut the friction. Consolidated the original two-CTA module into a single clear CTA with product-type selection and ZIP — fewer decisions, less visual weight, same intent capture.

  • $2.79M: projected annualized value from the simplified variant
  • Lower friction: visual and interaction friction reduced vs the original Winner #1
  • Durable: the concept works across design implementations — intent is the driver

Meta-learning: don't just roll out the first winner — retest its components. You'll either confirm the design is load-bearing, or (as here) find a simpler version that ships better.

Winner #2 — Home Hero CTA + Product Grid

Audience. Returning visitors hitting Home — often high-intent and further along the consideration curve than Product-page entrants.

Hypothesis. Replacing the generic hero with a prominent CTA + a product grid of the full UHOne portfolio would raise Quote Starts for returning users and reduce bounce for new users who needed a product-level orientation.

Result. $2.63M projected annualized incremental value. The grid reduced the cognitive cost of "which UHOne product do I need?" — a question the control forced users to answer implicitly. Even a traffic-hungry Home page benefits from more product-first signal, especially for audiences past initial consideration.

Winner #3 — "Why United?" Module on Product Pages

Hypothesis. Product pages attract high volumes of new visitors. Adding a module that differentiates UHOne from competitors on these pages would build enough trust on the first visit to bring users back — and return visits are where they convert.

Results (95% stat sig):

  • +4.02%: Quote Start Rate
  • +6.4%: Quote Submit Rate
  • +26%: Return Visit Rate

The Quote Start Rate lift compounded because module-exposed visitors were much more likely to return — and return visits converted at higher rates.

Takeaway. Top-of-funnel trust content belongs on Product pages. It doesn't compete with intent capture — it reinforces it.

Key Learnings

Six learnings travel from this program to the next:

  1. Intent capture is the highest-leverage lever on UHOne. Capturing product type + ZIP at the top of the page drove the largest individual test win ($5.95M) and underpinned the simplified variant ($2.79M). The concept generalizes across placements.
  2. Guardrails stayed green. Across every winning test, downstream Census completion and bounce held or improved. We moved the top of the funnel without breaking the bottom.
  3. Design is iteratable; the bet isn't. The simplified Sticky Nav retest showed the intent-capture bet survives design changes. Future work should treat placement, copy and density as variables around a fixed strategic thesis.
  4. Upper-funnel trust compounds. The "Why United?" module created return visits, not just first-visit conversions. For new-visitor-heavy pages, trust content is a conversion asset — on a delay.
  5. Audit-first beats idea-first. Every winner traced back to a specific finding in the initial audit. The pipeline's job was to translate evidence into tests, not to generate ideas from scratch.
  6. Productionize fast, or lose the lift. Winning tests migrated from the testing platform to AEM quickly. Speed to production converted statistical wins into real annualized value.

What's Next

Extending the thesis from capture to conversion across three time horizons:

  • Now / next 30 days: Finalize AEM migration for Simplified Sticky Nav across all product verticals (dental, vision, short-term, HPG, STM, accident); launch second-round Why-United module redesign for new-visitor-heavy product pages; stand up experiment-results automation so every shipped win gets an executive-ready summary within 24 hours.
  • Next / 30–90 days: Use captured product-intent signal to personalize post-Census product display and hero creative; re-test Home Hero CTA + Product Grid against a layout optimized specifically for new visitors; build a modular testing framework for Census-adjacent components (entry point, product intent, ZIP surfacing).
  • Later / 90+ days: Extend intent-capture thesis into mobile-specific variants — Desktop was this program's focus; mobile is its own canvas. Connect CRO tests to MarTech: sync captured product intent to email/paid retargeting to convert return visits faster. Expand the pipeline from acquisition-funnel CRO into retention and cross-sell for existing UHOne members.
